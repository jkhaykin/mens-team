Process

Erotic Motivation

Our deepest sexual fantasies: that which is in plain sight, yet so well hidden.

What is it that we want, and why do we want it?

Why This Process

We’ve done processes that turned into our sexual histories. This process is different. It’s about reaching for the edge of what you’re willing to admit about your erotic engine: what drives you sexually, and what is erotic to you.

You may say, “I don’t want to share that with men. That’s what I do with women.” Fine. We’re not trying to get you off. But this process is an opportunity to step back and examine what gets you off, intimately, with other men with whom you probably have something in common.

According to Sigmund Freud, there are only two basic drives that motivate all thoughts, emotions, and behavior. Simply put, they are sex and aggression, also called Eros and Thanatos, or life and death. Freud believed they underlie every motivation we experience as humans.

Debunking Freud is currently in fashion. But consider how much of our conversation centers on kink and sex, which often seems to come down to expressing aggression (power) sexually. Yet we spend very little time looking at this energy directly. Nine times out of ten we make a joke for some comic relief. What exactly are we trying to get relief from in these jokes?

This process is a difficult one. It may feel deeper than we really want to go, too personal, or too complicated. That’s exactly why it’s worth doing.

The Process

Look Past the Forms to the Why

This process picks up after the specifics of what you’re into. The forms are boring details. This process wants to know the why behind those forms: what exactly are you feeling when you invoke them?

For example:

  • Why latex? What is latex? Clean, sterile, plastic. Taking a woman out of the package, like unwrapping an Apple product.
  • Why paying for sex? Paid. Don’t have to explain. You can take without guilt. Don’t have to be PC.

Why that choice? Why not bunny rabbits?

You may not have looked at this question very deeply yet. That’s OK. Just bring the question. Try to “tell on yourself” as completely as you can.

Lead Us to the Room Where It Happens

Help us find the pattern. Connect the dots as best you can. Then sit back and let the other men feel that space with you.

This is the kind of process where the reverse shares can be substantial. We will likely see ourselves in each other.

Be as “Incorrect” as Possible

Sex in the modern world is almost entirely hidden in terms of its drivers, because we can’t admit the drivers. They can feel violent, unacceptable, toxic. Our best release is often when we can let in the forbidden. Sex is almost always framed as naughty; we blush and make a joke, and it goes unexplained.

This process is a chance to reverse all those limitations. Swing for the fences. Be as “incorrect” as possible.

Separate “policy” from “accounting.” This is about how you feel, not what you propose.

Guidelines

  • Not sexual histories. Not where you happen to drive your car, but what makes your motor run.
  • What gets you off. Only you know your body. What does it tell you in the moment of passion?
  • What doesn’t. This process is just as much about what makes you go soft, and what does not work for you.
  • Listen to your body. What does it tell you every time you have sex, in both positive and negative ways?
  • Notice the jokes. We make a lot of jokes about sex. What is behind them? What are we getting relief from?
  • Fantasy specifics are welcome as a way in, as long as you keep reaching for the why.
